Section 1
Headline + Messaging
Current Copy — marketing.html
Eyebrow: "Golf Tournament Platform · By Greenskeeper Studios"
H1: "Your Tournament. Your Brand. One Platform."
Hero sub: "Live scoring, player registration, team management, and a branded PWA — for any golf tournament."
Bottom CTA headline: "Stop Running Your Tournament on a Spreadsheet."
CTA sub: "Starts at $3/month. Up and running in 15 minutes. Less than a sleeve of balls."
Analysis
  • H1 is strong. "Your Tournament. Your Brand. One Platform." is punchy, audience-specific, and passes the 5-second test. Grade: A.
  • Eyebrow nails the positioning. "Golf Tournament Platform" removes ambiguity immediately. No "Bova Invitational" branding leak — the page correctly presents TOURney as a platform, not a single-event site.
  • Meta description is feature-list, not benefit-first. marketing.html:15: "Live scoring, player registration, team management, and a branded PWA" tells what it does, not what the organizer gains (time saved, player experience, professional look).
  • Bottom CTA headline is the best line on the page. "Stop Running Your Tournament on a Spreadsheet" is direct pain-point marketing. It should be closer to the hero or tested as the H1.
  • Missing: No tagline that lives independently of the H1. Nothing quotable on social. "Less than a sleeve of balls" is fun but buried in the bottom CTA where few visitors scroll.
Recommendations
  1. Swap hero sub to outcome-first: "Your players get a live-scoring PWA with your logo. You get an admin panel that runs the whole thing from your phone."
  2. Move "Stop Running Your Tournament on a Spreadsheet" up — either as an eyebrow variant or a section header between hero and features.
  3. Add a tagline beneath the TOURney wordmark in the nav: "Golf Tournament Software" — helps SEO and scanner comprehension.

Feature Presentation
What's Shown (marketing.html feature grid)
  • Live Leaderboard — "Real-time hole-by-hole scoring… Scramble, stroke play, best ball, stableford"
  • 100% Your Brand — "Your logo, your colors, your tournament name on every screen"
  • Multi-Day Events — "Up to 7 days. Different format each day."
  • Player Registration — "Waitlist, player directory. Your players sign up, you manage the list."
  • Demo section — embedded live app (Leaderboard / Scorecard / Feed tabs) with animated phone in hero
  • How It Works — 3-step wizard (Build → Share → Play)
Features vs Benefits Ratio

~70% feature statements, ~30% benefit framing. The feature grid titles are noun-based ("Live Leaderboard", "Multi-Day Events") rather than outcome-based ("Players know the standings in real time", "Run a week-long event without a spreadsheet").

What's NOT called out (but should be):

  • PWA install — players add it to their home screen, zero app store friction. This is a huge differentiator vs Golf Genius but never named as "installable app."
  • Admin panel from phone — the organizer never needs a laptop. Not mentioned.
  • Teams/pairs/individual in same tournament — genuinely rare. Buried in feature body copy, not headline.
  • White-label ("players never see TOURney") — mentioned but undersold. This is a premium signal.
  • Scoring format flexibility (stableford, stroke, scramble, best ball) — mentioned once inline, not a headline feature.
Demo Section — Strength

The embedded live demo (marketing.html "See It In Action") is a genuine differentiator. "No redirects. No sign-up required. This is the actual app your players will use." This should be promoted harder — it's the single best trust-builder on the page and most visitors probably miss it below the fold.

No static screenshots or hero product shot — the animated phone mock is CSS-only, not a real screenshot. This is acceptable for MVP but a real screenshot at key resolution (375px iPhone) would convert better.

Section 4
Pricing + Conversion Funnel
Pricing Visibility
  • Landing page: "Starts at $3/month" in the bottom CTA sub. Pricing section exists on the page with tier cards. Good
  • Comparison callout: "Golf Genius charges $1,300/year. TOURney is a fraction of the cost and fully self-serve." — direct and effective. Strong
  • create.html: Tier cards shown in the creation wizard with pricing and features. Present
  • Free tier: Not mentioned on the landing page. If one exists, it needs prominent placement. If not, "15-minute setup" + low price point softens the commitment.
  • Annual vs monthly toggle: Not seen on landing page. If annual discount exists, show it — reduces perceived price.
Funnel Steps
  1. Landing page (/) → Browse features + pricing
  2. "Build Your Tournament →" → /create wizard
  3. Account creation (friction point — Supabase auth)
  4. Tier selection (pricing shown again)
  5. Stripe checkout
  6. Admin panel + share URL

The funnel is clean at 6 steps. Step 3 (account creation before any product preview) is the highest drop-off risk. Consider showing the wizard UI before auth, then requiring signup to save/publish.

Good Page Weight / Load Speed
marketing.html is a single self-contained file with inlined CSS. External deps: Google Fonts only (2 families). No third-party scripts. No tracking pixels. Page should load in under 1s on 3G. This is solid for a marketing page.

No <link rel="canonical"> tag. No JSON-LD structured data (SoftwareApplication or Product schema). Missing structured data means Google can't surface rich results (star ratings, pricing) in SERPs. Low priority for MVP but worth adding pre-launch.

The H1 "Your Tournament. Your Brand. One Platform." functions as a de facto tagline but isn't surfaced consistently. The nav logo shows only "TOURney" with no supporting descriptor. The footer shows "© 2026 Greenskeeper Studios" — no tagline.

Recommendation: lock in a single tagline ("Golf Tournament Software" or "Run Your Tournament Like a Pro") and place it consistently in the nav wordmark, footer, og:title, and any print/email assets.

Premium Feel Rating

The contact email (rob@greenskeeper.studio) in the pricing section is a personal touch but also signals solo founder — could cut both ways.
